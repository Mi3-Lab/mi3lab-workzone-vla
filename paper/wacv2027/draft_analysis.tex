\section{Failure Analysis: the Ego-Relevance Gap}
\label{sec:analysis}

Nearly all residual false-positive mass shares one signature: the model's description is \emph{factually correct} about objects that are \emph{irrelevant to the vehicle's path}.  Representative validation cases: a parked work vehicle on the roadside 60+\,m away (\textsc{desc}: ``Work vehicle parked on right side of road''---true, and \textsc{outside} in ground truth); sidewalk reconstruction behind barriers (``Barriers on right sidewalk''); and residual signage for a different street's closure (``\textsc{end detour}'').  The lexical qualifier filter catches the subset that the model happens to verbalize with a qualifier, but the model does not \emph{reliably} emit qualifiers, and binary or distance-based prompts cannot elicit the judgment (\cref{sec:prompts}).

The natural reading of this is a \emph{fine-tuning coverage} problem: our curriculum's hard-negative stage drove clean-scene errors to zero, but negative examples of \emph{empty} scenes teach nothing about scenes whose work-zone objects are real and merely irrelevant.  A control experiment shows that reading is incomplete.  We repeated the ego-relevance probes on Cosmos3-Edge, a 4B mixture-of-transformers world model released for physical AI and quantized to INT4 for the same device (\cref{sec:worldmodel}).  This model is \emph{not} weak at perception in our setting: it transcribes signage correctly, emits well-formed 2D boxes for cones and barriers, and detects work zones in out-of-domain night, rain, and fog footage where our fine-tuned checkpoint fails almost completely (\cref{tab:ood}).  Asked whether the work-zone objects lie on the ego lane, an adjacent lane, or off-road, it answers ``off-road/sidewalk'' for the majority of frames in which the vehicle is verifiably \emph{inside} a work zone---the same failure, with the same sign, as our own model.  Its answers are close to independent of the ground truth.

Grounding the same question in geometry rather than language does not rescue it either.  Building an ego corridor and testing it against the model's \emph{own} boxes inherits the affirmation bias of the query that produced them: the instruction ``locate all work-zone objects'' presupposes that such objects exist, and the model complies even on clean scenes (6/6 false positives on pure negatives).  A corridor cannot correct a bias in its own input.

We therefore restate the conclusion more strongly than a claim about our checkpoint.  Perceiving work-zone objects correctly and judging whether they \emph{claim the ego vehicle's path} are distinct capabilities, and the second is not reachable by prompting a model that has the first---across two model families, two parameter scales, and one model explicitly trained as a physical-AI world model.  The gap is a property of the interface, not of one fine-tuning run.

The same gap explains the one metric on which every system we measured fails: \textsc{exiting} (IoU 0.085 VLM, 0.106 paired detector, 0.071 world model), intrinsically ego-relative---objects still visible but receding---which neither counting detections nor a detection-flavored question captures; our \textsc{ego} head, offered ``exiting'' explicitly, never produces it.

Both point the same way, and now with a concrete instrument: supervision that grounds state in the vehicle's trajectory.  ROADWork's 129K path-annotated images~\cite{ghosh2025roadwork}, unused by our fine-tuning, would let ego-relevance labels be derived from recorded trajectories (does the path pass within $d$ meters of the object?) rather than a lexicon.  Cosmos3-Edge additionally \emph{generates} ego trajectories at inference time, which would supply the same signal without retraining; we measured this path to be correct but not yet real-time on our device (\cref{sec:worldmodel}), and leave closing the loop as the principal direction for future work.
