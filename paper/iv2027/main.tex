\title{The Hidden Variable in Embedded VLM Perception: Temporal Calibration for Real-Time Work-Zone State Estimation}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}\IEEEauthorblockA{Paper under double-blind review}}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Vision--language models (VLMs) are increasingly proposed as replacements for detector pipelines in driving perception, and such comparisons usually keep the temporal smoothing constants each system was published with.  We show on embedded hardware that this hidden variable can decide the outcome.  We deploy an INT4 2B VLM fine-tuned on $\sim$46K work-zone question--answer pairs and $\sim$10K hard negatives and a zero-shot 4B physical-AI reasoner on a Jetson AGX Orin, operate both through an evidence-first prompt cascade, and compare them with a YOLO+CLIP pipeline under a latency-honest protocol in which each system's measured latency determines frame dropping.  On 484K annotated frames of ROADWork driving, constants inherited from the 2B leave the 4B model at macro-F1 0.290; recalibrated, it reaches 0.515.  The detector baseline gains too: recalibration lifts it from 0.470 to 0.551, the best result, and no VLM-based system then beats it.  A counted hidden-Markov filter on the detector score alone matches the recalibrated pipeline without a threshold search.  These conclusions hold on drive-disjoint folds, at matched false-alarm rates and with matched search budgets, and our replay is checked against live concurrent execution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Work zones concentrate difficult driving-perception conditions: their appearance is regional and unstandardized, geometry changes daily, evidence such as cones, barrels, signs, and workers is small or occluded, and both misses and nuisance alerts are costly.  They accounted for roughly 101{,}000 U.S. crashes and 899 fatalities in 2023~\cite{nhtsa2023}, yet production ADAS rarely model work zones explicitly and temporary speed limits are often absent from digital maps.

The strongest onboard approach combines a YOLO-style work-zone detector, CLIP verification~\cite{radford2021clip}, exponential smoothing, and hysteresis to estimate four traversal states~\cite{priorsystem2026,priorsystemarxiv2026}.  It is fast and effective after a zone is established, but reported IoU falls from 0.56/0.47 on \textsc{outside}/\textsc{inside} to 0.11/0.09 on \textsc{approaching}/\textsc{exiting}~\cite{priorsystem2026}.  These boundaries are precisely where an advisory is most useful: an alert after the vehicle reaches the barrels has little anticipatory value.

Meanwhile, fine-tuned VLMs describe work-zone scenes well offline~\cite{ghosh2025roadwork}, and weight-only quantization plus embedded runtimes bring small VLMs to automotive hardware~\cite{lin2024awq,trtedgellm}.  Whether they should replace detector pipelines is usually judged by comparing systems as published, each with the smoothing and hysteresis constants its authors tuned.  We build the strongest embedded VLM estimator we can, run it beside the detector pipeline on the same device, videos and protocol, and ask what such a comparison actually measures.

The answer is: mostly the temporal constants (\cref{fig:ranking}).  Against the hand-tuned detector pipeline, our VLM-based systems look competitive or better.  But constants inherited from one checkpoint leave a 4B reasoner at \textsc{inside} IoU 0.007, and its own calibration lifts it to 0.574; the same calibration applied to the detector pipeline raises its macro-F1 from 0.470 to 0.551, after which no VLM-based system beats it, and a counted filter on the detector score alone matches it without a search.  These conclusions survive drive-disjoint folds, matched false-alarm rates and matched search budgets, and our replay is checked against a live concurrent run.  A team choosing a perception stack from the usual comparison would adopt a costlier VLM stack for no measurable gain.

At $\sim$10\,ms per output token, a description costs $\sim$500\,ms and a two-token answer 75\,ms, so our cascade lets evidence quality gate smoothing (\cref{sec:prompts}): a transcribed sign can bypass a vote while a binary answer cannot.

Our contributions are three.  \textbf{(1) Evaluation:} a latency-honest protocol in which measured cycle time drives frame dropping, checked against live concurrent execution, with per-model recalibration by evidence replay.  \textbf{(2) Findings:} temporal constants do not transfer between the systems we test, and recalibrating the detector baseline changes the ranking: the joint estimator beats the hand-tuned pipeline ($+0.076$) but not the calibrated one, and no VLM-based system does; this holds on drive-disjoint folds, at matched false alarms and with matched search budgets.  \textbf{(3) Systems:} an evidence-first cascade that runs a quantized 2B VLM in real time on the Orin, an INT4 deployment of the Cosmos3-Edge reasoner on the same device, and a counted hidden-Markov filter that matches the recalibrated baseline from the detector score alone, with an analysis that locates the residual VLM gap in ego-relevance.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Work-zone perception.} Early systems used structured environmental models with sensor fusion~\cite{shi2021workzone} or frame-level classification~\cite{abodo2018shrp}.  ROADWork~\cite{ghosh2025roadwork} contains 4{,}375 videos and 9.6K annotated images across 18 U.S.\ cities, and shows that open-vocabulary models improve sharply after in-domain fine-tuning (+32.2 AP for detection and +36.7 SPICE for description).  The closest system~\cite{priorsystem2026} deploys a ROADWork-trained YOLO detector with CLIP verification, EMA smoothing, and hysteresis, and defines the traversal-state evaluation we adopt.

\paragraph{VLMs and world models for driving.} Large VLMs support end-to-end planning~\cite{hwang2024emma,tian2024drivevlm}, open-loop description~\cite{marcu2024lingoqa}, and reasoning~\cite{nvidia2025cosmosreason}, and Cosmos world models plan at datacenter or automotive-SoC scale~\cite{nvidia2026cosmos3,alpamayo2026}.  We instead estimate one safety-relevant scene state under an embedded latency budget.

\paragraph{Efficient inference and cascades.} AWQ~\cite{lin2024awq} and embedded runtimes~\cite{trtedgellm} enable multi-Hz VLM inference on embedded GPUs.  Detection cascades~\cite{viola2001rapid} and LLM cascades~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt} chain models of increasing cost and escalate on scalar confidence.  Our stages share one model: prompts and token budgets create the cost asymmetry, and evidence semantics (transcribed text versus a binary answer) drive escalation.

\paragraph{Temporal filtering and fair comparison.} Our joint estimator is a discrete hidden Markov model~\cite{rabiner1989hmm} run as a Bayes forward filter~\cite{thrun2005probabilistic}, with tables estimated by counting; we claim no novelty for the filter.  That baselines need the same tuning budget as the proposed method is known~\cite{melis2018state}; we measure how much it changes conclusions for embedded temporal estimators.

\section{System}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Two embedded deployments}
\label{sec:deployment}

We deploy \emph{two} models on the same Jetson AGX Orin 64\,GB, which shares its SoC with the automotive DRIVE AGX Orin, adapted in the two ways such a model admits: one by task fine-tuning, one by quantization and per-model calibration alone.  Both run the identical prompt channels, cascade, protocol and splits, so every comparison in this paper varies the model and its adaptation, never the pipeline around it.

\textbf{Fine-tuned 2B.}  A 2B-parameter VLM (Qwen3-VL architecture~\cite{qwen3vl}, initialized from Cosmos-Reason2-2B~\cite{nvidia2025cosmosreason2}), used for perception only.  We fine-tune it through a ROADWork-derived curriculum~\cite{ghosh2025roadwork}: work-zone VQA ($\sim$46K pairs), ego-state supervision, sign reading, a general-driving retention mix, and $\sim$10K work-zone-free BDD100K hard negatives.  Before the last stage, the existence gate answered ``yes'' on 95\% of clean scenes; after it, 0\% on external negative controls (clean BDD100K scenes; on ROADWork \textsc{outside} seconds, which include visible but irrelevant work, it is far less selective, \cref{tab:bias}).

The language backbone is quantized to INT4 (W4A16 AWQ, group size 128), the vision tower stays FP16, and both are compiled to TensorRT engines on-device~\cite{trtedgellm} (1.38\,GB language + 0.83\,GB vision).  Decoding costs 10.4\,ms per output token and dominates, followed by vision encoding (44.6\,ms at 720p, $\sim$20\,ms at the 480px we use).

\textbf{Quantized 4B world model.}  Cosmos3-Edge (C3E)~\cite{nvidia2026cosmos3} is a 4B physical-AI world model pairing an autoregressive reasoner with a trajectory-generating diffusion transformer.  We use only the reasoner; the diffusion transformer is never run, and ``world model'' below refers to this reasoner.  We quantize it to W4A16 AWQ INT4 (1.27\,GB) and keep its visual tower in FP16 (983\,MB), both as TensorRT engines on the same Orin; its frozen $26{\times}46$ patch grid fixes inputs at $736{\times}416$, and channel latencies are 162/182/488/556\,ms (\textsc{gate}/\textsc{ego}/\textsc{desc}/\textsc{sign}).  It receives no work-zone training data and has never seen this benchmark.  Building its engines required six patches to the embedded runtime; the patch set and deployment recipe are released with the code.

\subsection{Prompt channels and their failure modes}
\label{sec:prompts}

We operate four fixed-prompt channels with fixed token budgets.  Because the first generated token is an answer-start token, the minimum useful budget is two tokens.

\paragraph{Affirmation bias.} The \textsc{gate} asks a neutral existence question and is calibrated on clean scenes, but binary prompts cannot be specialized by rephrasing.  Variants asking whether work affects the vehicle's path all answered ``yes'' on \textsc{outside} probes, while distance prompts answered ``close'' regardless of scene.  The learned discrimination resides in the trained question; prompt nuance does not transfer.  Binary outputs are therefore weak evidence requiring a temporal vote, never sole triggers.

\paragraph{Faithful transcription.} The \textsc{sign} channel returns stable, verbatim text such as ``\textsc{shoulder work}'' and correctly reports absence.  A binary rephrasing misses signs that transcription catches on the same frames.  We therefore transcribe and derive a binary value by matching a work-zone keyword lexicon.

\paragraph{Structured corroboration.} The \textsc{desc} channel's object--location prefix often verbalizes disqualifying context---e.g., ``barriers on right \emph{sidewalk}'' or ``work vehicle \emph{parked}.''  Corroboration therefore requires a work-zone object without such a qualifier.  Prompts, budgets and lexicons are released with the code.

\subsection{Evidence-first cascade}
\label{sec:cascade}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_cascade.pdf}
\caption{Evidence-first cascade.  In \textsc{outside}, \textsc{gate} and \textsc{sign} run every cycle and candidate evidence invokes \textsc{desc}.  Entry requires a sustained, corroborated vote or high-confidence same-cycle evidence.  In active states, \textsc{ego} drives a Bayesian filter; sustained gate clearance returns to \textsc{outside}, while partial fading triggers \textsc{exiting}.  Constants are calibrated per model: $5/3/4/2$ for the 2B and $3/3/2/1$ for C3E, so the structure transfers but the numbers do not.  Diagram values are the 2B's, with rounded latencies (measured values in \cref{sec:cost}).}
\label{fig:cascade}
\end{figure}

The cascade (\cref{fig:cascade}) allocates compute by state.

\textbf{Outside (patrol).}  \textsc{gate} and \textsc{sign} run every cycle (2B at 480px: p95 259\,ms, 3.9\,Hz, \cref{sec:cost}); either can invoke \textsc{desc}.  Entry into \textsc{approaching} has two paths: a voting path, with the gate active in at least $K_\text{enter}$ of the last $N$ cycles and one corroboration, or fast entry on a lexicon-matching sign or gate+\textsc{desc} agreement in the same cycle.  Fast entry avoids a voting delay of up to $N$ cycles ($\sim$1.3\,s for the 2B) for evidence such as a transcribed ``\textsc{road work ahead}.''

\textbf{Active states.}  \textsc{ego} updates a Bayesian filter whose argmax is restricted to the three active states.  Otherwise, a single ``outside'' reply immediately after sign-triggered entry would erase the sign's anticipatory value.  Only sustained gate clearance returns the system to \textsc{outside}; partial fading triggers \textsc{exiting}, which \textsc{ego} never produced even when offered explicitly.  \textsc{desc} refreshes every 1.5\,s.  For the 2B, the thresholds ($N{=}5$, $K_\text{enter}{=}3$, $K_\text{exit}{=}4$, $K_\text{fade}{=}2$) were fixed on calibration data; C3E gets its own (\cref{sec:worldmodel}).  With \textsc{ego} disabled, as selected for C3E, the filter's emission is uniform: \textsc{approaching} becomes \textsc{inside} on the transition prior alone, 13 cycles after entry, and \textsc{exiting} and \textsc{outside} still come from the fade and clearance votes.

\subsection{Joint estimator over both evidence streams}
\label{sec:jointdesign}

Combining the detector and the world model by grafting one onto the other's temporal machinery does not work.  The ported detector counts cycles (a 150-cycle maximum approach duration, 20 minimum frames outside) tuned for its native loop; the cascade votes over a window tuned for the 2B's $\sim$260\,ms cycle, while C3E answers only every $\sim$1.5\,s alongside the detector.  Reusing either as the spine imports constants that are wrong for the combination, the same problem as inheriting thresholds across models (\cref{sec:worldmodel}), one level up.

The joint estimator therefore inherits no constant from either pipeline: it has no EMA, no hysteresis threshold and no cycle counter, and its tables are counted; its remaining design choices (bucket edges and the evidence rate $\tau$ below) were set on calibration data (\cref{sec:joint}).  It is a discrete hidden Markov model over the four states, run as a forward filter~\cite{rabiner1989hmm}, with maximum-likelihood tables.  Its observation combines the detector's calibrated frame score (six buckets), the world model's gate, sign-keyword and corroboration answers ordered by evidence strength (six levels), and the age of that answer (three buckets), for 108 discrete cells.  The emission $P(\text{observation}\mid\text{state})$ is counted once per detector cycle on the calibration split with Laplace smoothing, and it is joint rather than factorized because the interaction is the signal: the world model's \textsc{approaching} is reliable when the detector corroborates it and mostly a false alarm when it does not (73\% of those frames are \textsc{outside}).  The transition is a per-second rate matrix, counted as ground-truth transitions over dwell time and scaled by the elapsed $dt$ of each step, so it does not depend on the loop rate.  Finally, the log-likelihood is scaled by $dt/\tau$ with $\tau=0.5$\,s: a world-model answer persists across roughly 30 detector frames, and applying its likelihood once per frame treats 30 correlated looks as independent, which drove an early version to 135 false alarms per hour.

At run time the detector runs at its native rate and is never blocked.  The world model is polled asynchronously and re-polled as soon as it answers; its last answer is held, with its age as part of the observation.  In our runs both models execute in one process, each charged its own measured latency on the simulated clock; we record the raw evidence with these clocks once per video and replay the filter on it, so ages and frame drops in every reported result are the measured ones.  \Cref{sec:joint} checks this replay against a live concurrent run.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Benchmark.} We use the detector baseline's 520-video ROADWork-derived traversal benchmark~\cite{priorsystem2026}: Boston and Seattle footage at 1920$\times$1080, 30\,fps, with 484K interval-labeled frames.  A state-stratified 60/40 video split assigns all design choices and thresholds to calibration (312 videos, 2.59\,h) and final results to validation (208 videos; 192{,}135 frames, 1.78\,h, 13 pure-negative videos); the benchmark totals 4.37\,h of annotated driving.  All systems see the same split.  All 25 drives occur in both partitions, so they are not route-independent; \cref{sec:dd} therefore repeats every replay-fitted comparison with drive-disjoint folds.  Fine-tuning images and benchmark videos both derive from ROADWork, a condition the ROADWork-trained baseline shares.

\paragraph{Latency-honest camera simulation.} Embedded perception cannot pause the world.  After each measured inference cycle of duration $\Delta$ on the target hardware, the replay advances by $\Delta$ and drops intervening frames exactly as a live camera would.  Slower systems therefore observe fewer, sparser frames and pay latency inside the accuracy result rather than beside it.  Predictions are held between cycles and scored per frame.

\paragraph{Metrics.} We adopt the baseline's suite~\cite{priorsystem2026}: frame accuracy and macro-F1; per-state IoU; event precision/recall, where an interval is correct if it overlaps one of the same state; matched-event entry offset (Entry MAE: mean absolute offset over all states; \textsc{in} entry med.: median absolute offset of \textsc{inside} entries); and false alarms per hour, the rate of predicted active intervals overlapping no annotated active interval.  Videos beginning in a state contribute no entry event.  We keep the baseline's overlap rule for comparability and add a lead metric for timing (\cref{sec:vsdetector}).  Event metrics capture whether a warning occurs, IoU and timing how well it is placed.  Confidence intervals are paired 95\% cluster-bootstrap intervals (10{,}000 replications) that resample drives, so that correlated videos of one drive are drawn together; video-level intervals are reported where they differ in sign.

\subsection{Operational cost}
\label{sec:cost}

At 480px, p50/p95 latency is 83/90\,ms (\textsc{gate}), 136/149\,ms (\textsc{ego}), 155/169\,ms (\textsc{sign}), and 468/508\,ms (\textsc{desc}).  Patrol and active cycles run at p95 259\,ms (3.9\,Hz) and 239\,ms (4.2\,Hz); the heaviest path is 768\,ms (1.3\,Hz).  Here ``real time'' means keeping pace with a live camera at these rates (at 30\,m/s, a 259\,ms cycle is 7.8\,m of travel), not meeting a specific alert deadline.  Measured above idle with tegrastats, the VLM draws 28.6\,W and 3--26\,J per cycle, the detector 22.4\,W and 0.8\,J at 24\,ms.

\section{Calibrating Every System}
\label{sec:worldmodel}

\Cref{sec:results} confounded each checkpoint with thresholds fitted to the 2B.  We separate the two by recalibrating every system on its own evidence, which changes two conclusions above.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_timeline.pdf}
\caption{Ground truth and five systems on one validation video, chosen to illustrate the effect; thumbnails mark the middle of each annotated state.  With the 2B's constants, C3E never leaves \textsc{approaching}; recalibrated, it tracks the zone.  The hand-tuned detector pipeline enters \textsc{inside} 5\,s early and cuts \textsc{exiting} short; recalibrated, it follows all four states.}
\label{fig:timeline}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Temporal hyper-parameters do not transfer.}  With the 2B thresholds, C3E scores macro-F1 0.290 and \textsc{inside} IoU 0.007 at 3.8\% recall.  This is an artifact, not an inability to recognize being inside a zone.  Recalibrating the same four integers for C3E moves these to 0.515, 0.574, and 87.0\% on validation: \textsc{inside} IoU rises 0.567 absolute and recall 83.2 points, with model, prompts and videos fixed (\cref{fig:timeline}).  The search covers $N\in\{3,5,7,9\}$, $K_\ast\in 1..N$ and the \textsc{ego}/\textsc{sign} switches (4{,}896 configurations), scored by macro-F1 on the 100 calibration videos with recorded VLM evidence.  The selected configuration ($N{=}3$, $K_{\text{enter}}{=}3$, $K_{\text{exit}}{=}2$, $K_{\text{fade}}{=}1$; calibration macro-F1 0.484) is shorter and less hysteretic, consistent with less noisy per-frame evidence, and disables \textsc{ego} (\cref{sec:analysis}).  Because it sat on the smallest $N$ of the grid, we later extended the grid to $N\in\{1,2,4\}$; the optimum did not change.  Recording each channel's output once per sampled frame and replaying the state machine turns a threshold sweep from GPU-hours per configuration into CPU-seconds.

The identical search on the identical videos moves the fine-tuned 2B by only +0.006 on validation (0.453$\to$0.459), against +0.225 for C3E: a search that merely rewarded flexibility would have helped both models equally.

\paragraph{The baseline too.}  The argument applies to the detector pipeline, whose ported state machine keeps constants hand-tuned for its native loop on a different split.  We recorded every component on every cycle of all 520 videos (detector counts before and after CLIP verification, the global CLIP score and the colour-context score) and replay the complete pipeline, keeping the CLIP and colour weights at the app's values; replaying the hand-tuned configuration reproduces the live run (0.473 vs.\ 0.470).  We searched its thresholds, EMA rate, minimum-outside count and maximum approach duration on the 312 calibration videos (three grids of 4{,}800 configurations), selecting on calibration macro-F1 alone.  Validation macro-F1 rises to 0.551 ($+0.077$, $[+0.039,+0.108]$; 0.486$\to$0.538 on calibration) with 92.5\% \textsc{inside} precision and 20.2 false alarms per hour (\cref{tab:main}), the strongest system we measure.  The same search on the detector score alone, without CLIP, gives 0.435$\to$0.530.

The same procedure also measures fine-tuning under matched conditions.  Recalibrating the \emph{base} checkpoint---identical family, scale, architecture and visual tower, differing only in the work-zone curriculum---lifts it from macro-F1 0.281 to 0.383 on the calibration videos used for the search, against 0.451 to 0.496 for the fine-tuned version.  Given their own thresholds, fine-tuning is worth $+0.113$ macro-F1 and $+8.8$ accuracy points, not the near-total collapse (\textsc{inside} IoU 0.535$\to$0.012) that the base checkpoint shows under the fine-tuned model's thresholds.  It buys a real and specific capability in domain.

Every decision in this paper is made on the 312-video calibration partition and none on validation: prompts, budgets and the qualifier lexicon on the full partition; VLM thresholds by replaying evidence recorded on a 100-video subset of it; the detector's thresholds and the counted filter's tables on all 312 videos.  The 208 validation videos enter only as the reported result.  Search budgets are therefore unequal, and they favour the detector: three grids over 312 videos against one over 100.  Given the VLM's budget, one grid on the same 100 videos, the recalibrated detector+CLIP pipeline reaches 0.522 on validation, still above its hand-tuned version ($+0.052$, $[+0.038,+0.070]$) and level with C3E ($-0.008$ for C3E, $[-0.050,+0.029]$).

\paragraph{No configuration dominates.}  Calibrated, the world model and the detector pipeline are statistically tied on macro-F1 ($-0.036$, $[-0.080,+0.005]$) and fail complementarily: C3E leads \textsc{inside} IoU (0.574 vs.\ 0.552) and entry MAE, the recalibrated detector \textsc{inside} precision and nuisance (20.2 vs.\ 64.6 false alarms/h).  \Cref{sec:joint} combines their evidence.

\section{Combining the Detector and the World Model}
\label{sec:joint}

The joint estimator of \cref{sec:jointdesign} reaches macro-F1 0.546 at 25.9 false alarms per hour (\cref{tab:main}).  Against the hand-tuned detector+CLIP pipeline it gains 0.076 macro-F1 ($[+0.038,+0.104]$); against the recalibrated pipeline the two are tied ($-0.005$, $[-0.046,+0.033]$), as are their \textsc{inside} IoU and false alarms, and the joint estimator trades \textsc{inside} event precision (66.8\% vs.\ 92.5\%) for recall (85.9\% vs.\ 77.8\%).  Against the calibrated world model it gains 0.031 macro-F1 ($[+0.006,+0.058]$) and 0.089 \textsc{exiting} IoU and removes 38.8 alarms per hour.  We treat the comparisons against the recalibrated detector as confirmatory and the per-metric differences as exploratory.

Every table is counted on all 312 calibration videos; 5-fold cross-validation within calibration gives 0.535 (s.d.\ 0.019).  The evidence rate trades accuracy for nuisance ($\tau{=}0.25$: 0.538 at 44.7 false alarms/h; $\tau{=}1$: 0.504 at 22.0/h); we keep $\tau{=}0.5$.  The tables are sparse but concentrated, 20 of the 108 cells carrying 97\% of the observations, and 3 or 10 detector buckets instead of 6 give 0.538 and 0.545.

\paragraph{Concurrent execution.}  In the recorded runs behind every reported result, the detector cycle took p50 44\,ms (p95 59\,ms) and the world model answered every 1.54\,s.  To check the replay, we ran both models truly concurrently on the Orin, in wall-clock time from a decoded camera stream, on 20 validation videos, twice.  Sharing the GPU raises the detector cycle from 24\,ms alone to 54\,ms (p95 59\,ms, 19\,Hz), while the world model answers every 1.44\,s.  Run live, the detector-only counted filter reproduces its replay on these videos (macro-F1 0.425 vs.\ 0.423); the joint estimator scores 0.44 in both live runs against 0.50 in replay, a paired difference ($-0.07$, $[-0.16,+0.01]$) that 20 videos cannot resolve.  The Orin draws 39.0\,W median (46.9\,W peak) with both models, against 33.5\,W for the detector alone and 11.1\,W idle.

\paragraph{Where the gain comes from.}  A channel ablation refits the filter without each stream.  Removing the world model entirely, including the age of its answer, costs 0.022 macro-F1 and lowers the alarm rate (25.9 to 22.5 per hour).  Removing the detector costs 0.086 macro-F1 and collapses \textsc{exiting} IoU from 0.160 to 0.017.  Of the world-model channels, \textsc{desc} contributes most.  The detector score alone inside the counted filter scores 0.524 at 22.5 false alarms per hour, tied with the recalibrated detector+CLIP pipeline on both ($-0.026$, $[-0.062,+0.009]$; $+2.2$/h, $[-8.1,+11.1]$) without CLIP or a threshold search, and with 11.2 fewer alarms per hour than the state machine recalibrated on the same score ($[-24.6,-2.1]$).  On this benchmark the most efficient option is therefore the detector inside the counted filter.  The world model's evidence is complementary where the detector's is weak: on the 10 validation onsets preceded by a transcribed work-zone sign, the joint estimator and C3E warn $\geq$2\,s ahead on all ten, against eight for the recalibrated detector+CLIP pipeline.

\paragraph{A learned alternative.}  A strictly causal three-stage TCN~\cite{farha2019mstcn} trained on the same evidence ties the best macro-F1 (0.550) but at 71.9 false alarms per hour.



\subsection{Drive-disjoint folds and matched false alarms}
\label{sec:dd}

To rule out route-specific calibration, we pool all 520 videos and split the 25 drives into five folds of about 104 videos.  Each replay-fitted system is refitted on the other four folds' drives only, each detector state machine with the full three-grid search, and replayed on the held-out fold, so no test video shares a drive with any video used to fit it (\cref{tab:dd}).  The conclusions of \cref{sec:worldmodel,sec:joint} hold: recalibration raises the detector+CLIP pipeline by 0.054 macro-F1 ($[+0.026,+0.078]$, drive-level) and the state machine on the detector score alone by 0.089 ($[+0.058,+0.114]$); the counted filters tie the recalibrated pipeline ($-0.010$, $[-0.036,+0.017]$, on the detector score alone; $+0.002$ with the world model), the detector-only filter at a similar alarm rate (22.5 vs.\ 18.7/h, $[-1.7,+8.8]$).  Restricted to the 208 validation videos, drive-disjoint fitting changes each system's macro-F1 by at most 0.010 from \cref{tab:main}.

Macro-F1 compares systems at different operating points, from 18 to 65 false alarms per hour.  We therefore pass every system through the same causal persistence filter, which shows an active interval only after it has lasted $d$ seconds, and increase $d$ until false alarms fall to 20/h (last column of \cref{tab:main,tab:dd}).  At matched nuisance, the recalibrated and counted systems lead: on drive-disjoint folds the recalibrated detector+CLIP pipeline keeps 0.535 at $d{=}0$, the joint estimator 0.523 and the counted detector filter 0.520 at $d{\leq}0.75$\,s, against 0.42 for the hand-tuned pipeline and for the state machine recalibrated on the detector score alone. \section{Failure Analysis: the Ego-Relevance Gap}
\label{sec:analysis}

Nearly all residual false-positive mass has one signature: descriptions are factually correct about objects irrelevant to the vehicle's path.  Examples include a parked work vehicle more than 60\,m away, sidewalk reconstruction behind barriers, and residual detour signs for another street.  The qualifier filter catches only cases where the model verbalizes this context; it does not emit qualifiers reliably, and binary or distance prompts do not recover the judgment.

\paragraph{Response bias.}  The three checkpoints were recorded on the same labeled videos, which separates how often a model says ``yes'' from how well its answer tracks the state (\cref{tab:bias}).  The base 2B rarely answers yes outside a zone and its gate separates \textsc{inside} from \textsc{outside} best (balanced accuracy 0.84); fine-tuning moves the same gate to 42.4\% and 0.75.  ROADWork's \textsc{outside} includes visible but irrelevant work, so a ``yes'' there is often correct perception and missing ego-relevance.  The fine-tuned model's advantage under matched calibration therefore does not come from its gate: its description corroborates on 10.7\% of \textsc{outside} seconds against 31.3\% for the base model.

Incomplete fine-tuning coverage is not the whole explanation.  C3E transcribes signs correctly and emits plausible 2D boxes, yet labels most verified \textsc{inside} frames ``off-road/sidewalk'' in ego-lane probes, nearly independently of ground truth.

Perceiving objects and judging whether they claim the ego path are distinct capabilities, and none of the prompts we tried bridged them in either model, across two scales and two adaptation strategies; the gap is not specific to one fine-tuning run.  It also explains weak \textsc{exiting} IoU for every language-only system: receding objects remain visible.  The joint estimator reaches 0.160 on \textsc{exiting} only through the detector: without it, \textsc{exiting} IoU falls to 0.017.  The detector's object score decays as objects recede, and the counted filter reads that decay directly; no language channel supplies it.

Trajectory-grounded supervision is the natural remedy.  ROADWork's 129K path annotations~\cite{ghosh2025roadwork}, unused in our fine-tuning, can derive ego-relevance labels from recorded trajectories rather than a lexicon.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our claims are bounded in four ways.  \emph{(i) Data:} fine-tuning and evaluation both use ROADWork, which covers two daytime U.S.\ cities; the drive-disjoint folds of \cref{sec:dd} address the replay-fitted temporal models, and the VLMs were not re-fine-tuned per fold.  \emph{(ii) Baseline:} every comparison uses our port of the detector pipeline, measured on our split and protocol (0.470; 0.600 published on the original split).  \emph{(iii) Search:} reported configurations are the best found on finite grids; the conclusions hold with matched budgets (\cref{sec:worldmodel}).  \emph{(iv) Scope:} replay is open-loop, and we claim computational feasibility, not safety readiness.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Comparing an embedded VLM with a detector pipeline as both were published measures their temporal constants as much as their perception.  On 484K annotated frames, constants inherited across models move \textsc{inside} IoU by 0.567, and recalibrating the detector baseline by the same procedure lifts it from 0.470 to 0.551, beyond the reach of every VLM-based system we built; a counted hidden-Markov filter on the detector score matches it without a search.  These results hold on drive-disjoint folds, at matched false alarms and with matched budgets, and the replay behind them is checked against live concurrent execution.  Embedded comparisons should therefore recalibrate every system, the baseline included, and compare at matched nuisance.  The VLMs' remaining gap is ego-relevance, which prompting did not close and trajectory-grounded supervision may.  Upon acceptance we release the replay harness, prompts, metrics and baseline port.
