\title{The Hidden Variable in Embedded VLM Perception: Temporal Calibration for Real-Time Work-Zone State Estimation}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}\IEEEauthorblockA{Paper under double-blind review}}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Vision--language models (VLMs) are increasingly proposed to replace detector pipelines in driving perception, and such comparisons usually keep each system's published temporal smoothing constants.  We show that these constants are a hidden variable that can decide the outcome.  We run a fine-tuned INT4 2B VLM and a zero-shot 4B physical-AI reasoner on a Jetson AGX Orin and compare them with a YOLO+CLIP pipeline on 484K annotated frames of ROADWork driving, under a latency-honest protocol in which measured latency drives frame dropping.  First, temporal constants do not transfer between models: inherited from the 2B, they leave the 4B model at macro-F1 0.290; recalibrated, it reaches 0.515.  Second, the baseline needs the same treatment: recalibrated, it rises from 0.470 to 0.551 and no VLM-based system beats it, while a counted hidden-Markov filter on its score alone is statistically tied with it.  These results hold on drive-disjoint folds and at matched false-alarm rates.  Third, cycle-counted constants do not transfer between devices either: on a DRIVE AGX Thor they cost the detector pipeline and the 4B model 0.03--0.04 macro-F1, in proportion to how much their cycle shrinks, while the per-second joint detector+C3E counted filter keeps its macro-F1.  Live execution reproduces the replay.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Work zones concentrate difficult driving-perception conditions: their appearance is regional and unstandardized, geometry changes daily, evidence such as cones, barrels, signs, and workers is small or occluded, and both misses and nuisance alerts are costly.  They accounted for roughly 101{,}000 U.S. crashes and 899 fatalities in 2023~\cite{nhtsa2023}, yet production ADAS rarely model work zones explicitly and temporary speed limits are often absent from digital maps.

The strongest onboard approach combines a YOLO-style work-zone detector, CLIP verification~\cite{radford2021clip}, exponential smoothing, and hysteresis to estimate four traversal states~\cite{priorsystem2026,priorsystemarxiv2026}.  It is fast and effective after a zone is established, but reported IoU falls from 0.56/0.47 on \textsc{outside}/\textsc{inside} to 0.11/0.09 on \textsc{approaching}/\textsc{exiting}~\cite{priorsystem2026}.  These boundaries are precisely where an advisory is most useful: an alert after the vehicle reaches the barrels has little anticipatory value.

Meanwhile, fine-tuned VLMs describe work-zone scenes well offline~\cite{ghosh2025roadwork}, and weight-only quantization plus embedded runtimes bring small VLMs to automotive hardware~\cite{lin2024awq,trtedgellm}.  Whether they should replace detector pipelines is usually judged by comparing systems as published, each with the smoothing and hysteresis constants its authors tuned.  We build the strongest embedded VLM estimator we can, run it beside the detector pipeline on the same device, videos and protocol, and ask what such a comparison actually measures.

The answer is: mostly the temporal constants (\cref{fig:ranking}).  Against the hand-tuned detector pipeline our VLM-based systems look competitive or better, but calibrating the baseline by the same procedure reverses the ranking.  A team choosing a perception stack from the usual comparison would adopt a costlier VLM stack for a gain that disappears once the baseline is calibrated.


Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Evaluation:} a latency-honest protocol with per-model recalibration by evidence replay, confirmed by live concurrent execution on the Orin;
\item \textbf{Findings:} temporal constants transfer neither between models nor between devices, and calibrating the baseline reverses the ranking; on unseen drives and at matched false alarms the VLM-based lead over the calibrated baseline disappears;
\item \textbf{Systems:} a real-time evidence-first cascade for a quantized 2B VLM, an INT4 Cosmos3-Edge reasoner on the same device, and a counted hidden-Markov filter with per-second parameters, whose joint detector+C3E variant keeps its macro-F1 on new hardware without recalibration, with an analysis of the VLMs' residual ego-relevance gap.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Work-zone perception.} Early systems used environmental models with sensor fusion~\cite{shi2021workzone} or frame classification~\cite{abodo2018shrp}.  ROADWork~\cite{ghosh2025roadwork} provides 4{,}375 videos and 9.6K annotated images from 18 U.S.\ cities and shows large gains from in-domain fine-tuning (+32.2 AP, +36.7 SPICE).  The closest system~\cite{priorsystem2026} deploys a ROADWork-trained YOLO detector with CLIP verification, EMA smoothing and hysteresis, and defines the traversal-state evaluation we adopt.

\paragraph{VLMs and world models for driving.} VLMs support planning~\cite{hwang2024emma,tian2024drivevlm}, description~\cite{marcu2024lingoqa} and reasoning~\cite{nvidia2025cosmosreason}, and Cosmos world models plan at datacenter or automotive-SoC scale~\cite{nvidia2026cosmos3,alpamayo2026}; we estimate one safety-relevant state under an embedded latency budget.  Unlike detection~\cite{viola2001rapid} and LLM~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt} cascades, which chain models of increasing cost and escalate on confidence, our stages share one model and escalate on evidence semantics (transcribed text versus a binary answer).

\paragraph{Temporal filtering and fair comparison.} Our joint estimator is a discrete hidden Markov model~\cite{rabiner1989hmm} run as a Bayes forward filter~\cite{thrun2005probabilistic}; we claim no novelty for it.  That baselines deserve the proposed method's tuning budget is known~\cite{melis2018state}; we measure how much it changes conclusions for embedded temporal estimators.

\section{System}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Two embedded deployments}
\label{sec:deployment}

We deploy \emph{two} models on the same Jetson AGX Orin 64\,GB, which shares its SoC with the automotive DRIVE AGX Orin, adapted in two common ways: one by task fine-tuning, one by quantization and per-model calibration alone.  Both run the same prompt channels, cascade structure, protocol and splits; only the model, its input size and its calibrated constants differ.

\textbf{Fine-tuned 2B.}  A 2B-parameter VLM (Qwen3-VL architecture~\cite{qwen3vl}, initialized from Cosmos-Reason2-2B~\cite{nvidia2025cosmosreason2}), used for perception only, fine-tuned through a curriculum derived from ROADWork~\cite{ghosh2025roadwork}, whose imagery spans 18 U.S.\ cities under one signing standard (MUTCD~\cite{mutcd2009}): work-zone VQA ($\sim$46K pairs), ego-state supervision, sign reading, a general-driving retention mix, and $\sim$10K work-zone-free BDD100K hard negatives.  The last stage brought the existence gate from 95\% to 0\% ``yes'' on clean BDD100K scenes (on ROADWork \textsc{outside} seconds, which include visible but irrelevant work, it is less selective, \cref{tab:bias}).

The language backbone is quantized to INT4 (W4A16 AWQ, group size 128) and the vision tower kept in FP16, both as on-device TensorRT engines~\cite{trtedgellm}.  Decoding (10.4\,ms/token) dominates cost, followed by vision encoding (44.6\,ms at 720p, $\sim$20\,ms at our 480px).

\textbf{Quantized 4B world model.}  Cosmos3-Edge (C3E)~\cite{nvidia2026cosmos3} pairs an autoregressive reasoner with a trajectory-generating diffusion transformer; we run only the reasoner, and ``world model'' below refers to it.  It is quantized to W4A16 INT4 with an FP16 visual tower on the same Orin; its frozen $26{\times}46$ patch grid fixes inputs at $736{\times}416$, and channel latencies are 162/182/488/556\,ms (\textsc{gate}/\textsc{ego}/\textsc{desc}/\textsc{sign}).  It has seen no work-zone training data.  Its engines required six patches to the embedded runtime, released upon acceptance.

\subsection{Prompt channels and their failure modes}
\label{sec:prompts}

We operate four fixed-prompt channels with fixed token budgets (the first generated token is an answer-start token).

\paragraph{Affirmation bias.} The \textsc{gate} asks a neutral existence question, but binary prompts cannot be specialized by rephrasing: path-relevance variants all answered ``yes'' on \textsc{outside} probes and distance prompts ``close'' regardless of scene.  Binary outputs are therefore weak evidence requiring a temporal vote, never sole triggers.  At $\sim$10\,ms per output token, a three-token answer costs $\sim$85\,ms and a description $\sim$500\,ms, so evidence quality, not only confidence, gates smoothing.

\paragraph{Faithful transcription.} The \textsc{sign} channel returns verbatim text such as ``\textsc{shoulder work}'' and correctly reports absence, while a binary rephrasing misses signs that transcription catches; we match transcripts against a work-zone lexicon.

\paragraph{Structured corroboration.} \textsc{desc} often verbalizes disqualifying context (``barriers on right \emph{sidewalk}'', ``work vehicle \emph{parked}''), so corroboration requires a work-zone object without such a qualifier.  Prompts, budgets and lexicons are released upon acceptance.

\subsection{Evidence-first cascade}
\label{sec:cascade}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{fig_cascade.pdf}
\caption{Evidence-first cascade.  In \textsc{outside}, \textsc{gate} and \textsc{sign} run every cycle and candidate evidence invokes \textsc{desc}.  Entry requires a sustained, corroborated vote or high-confidence same-cycle evidence.  In active states, \textsc{ego} drives a Bayesian filter; sustained gate clearance returns to \textsc{outside}, while partial fading triggers \textsc{exiting}.  Constants are calibrated per model: $5/3/4/2$ for the 2B and $3/3/2/1$ for C3E, so the structure transfers but the numbers do not.  Diagram values are the 2B's, with indicative latencies (measured values in \cref{sec:cost}).}
\label{fig:cascade}
\end{figure}

The cascade (\cref{fig:cascade}) allocates compute by state.

\textbf{Outside (patrol).}  \textsc{gate} and \textsc{sign} run every cycle (2B at 480px: p95 259\,ms, 3.9\,Hz), and either can invoke \textsc{desc}.  \textsc{approaching} is entered by vote (gate active in $K_\text{enter}$ of the last $N$ cycles plus one corroboration) or by fast entry on a lexicon-matching sign or same-cycle gate+\textsc{desc} agreement, which avoids a delay of up to $N$ cycles ($\sim$1.3\,s for the 2B) for evidence such as ``\textsc{road work ahead}.''

\textbf{Active states.}  \textsc{ego} updates a Bayesian filter whose argmax is restricted to the active states, so that one ``outside'' reply right after a sign-triggered entry cannot erase it.  Only sustained gate clearance returns to \textsc{outside}; partial fading triggers \textsc{exiting}, which \textsc{ego} never produced.  \textsc{desc} refreshes every 1.5\,s.  The 2B's thresholds ($N{=}5$, $K_\text{enter}{=}3$, $K_\text{exit}{=}4$, $K_\text{fade}{=}2$) were fixed on calibration data; C3E gets its own (\cref{sec:worldmodel}).  With \textsc{ego} disabled, as selected for C3E, \textsc{approaching} becomes \textsc{inside} on the transition prior alone, 13 cycles after entry.

\subsection{Joint estimator over both evidence streams}
\label{sec:jointdesign}

Grafting the detector and the world model onto each other's temporal machinery does not work: the ported detector counts cycles (150-cycle maximum approach, 20 minimum frames outside) tuned for its native loop, and the cascade votes over a window tuned for the 2B's $\sim$260\,ms cycle, while C3E answers only every $\sim$1.5\,s.  Either spine imports constants that are wrong for the combination.

The joint estimator therefore inherits no constant: it is a discrete hidden Markov model over the four states, run as a forward filter~\cite{rabiner1989hmm} with maximum-likelihood tables and no EMA, hysteresis threshold or cycle counter.  Its observation combines the detector's frame score (six buckets), the world model's gate, sign-keyword and corroboration answers ordered by strength (six levels), and the age of that answer (three buckets): 108 cells.  The emission is counted once per detector cycle on the calibration split with Laplace smoothing, jointly rather than factorized, because the interaction is the signal: the world model's \textsc{approaching} is reliable when the detector corroborates it and mostly a false alarm when it does not (73\% of those frames are \textsc{outside}).  The transition is a per-second rate matrix, counted as ground-truth transitions over dwell time and scaled by each step's $dt$, so it does not depend on the loop rate.  The log-likelihood is scaled by $dt/\tau$ ($\tau=0.5$\,s): a world-model answer persists across $\sim$30 detector frames, and counting it once per frame drove an early version to 135 false alarms per hour.  Bucket edges and $\tau$ were set on calibration data (\cref{sec:joint}).

The detector runs at its native rate and is never blocked; the world model is re-polled as soon as it answers, and its last answer is held with its age.  We record raw evidence with measured clocks once per video and replay the filter on it, so ages and frame drops in every result are measured ones; \cref{sec:joint} confirms the replay against live concurrent execution.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Benchmark.} We use the detector baseline's 520-video ROADWork traversal benchmark~\cite{priorsystem2026}: 4.31\,h of 1920$\times$1080 driving from 25 drives (30\,fps except seven 120\,fps videos), with 484K interval-labeled frames.  A state-stratified 60/40 split assigns all design choices to calibration (312 videos, 2.59\,h) and results to validation (208 videos, 192K frames, 1.73\,h, 13 pure-negative).  All 25 drives occur in both partitions, so \cref{sec:dd} repeats every replay-fitted comparison with drive-disjoint folds.  ROADWork samples its annotated images from the same drives as its videos, so the 2B's fine-tuning images, like the baseline detector's training images, may come from benchmark drives; C3E is zero-shot.  Every calibration effect we report compares a system with itself, perception fixed, and is not inflated by this overlap.

\paragraph{Latency-honest camera simulation.} After each measured inference cycle of duration $\Delta$ on the target hardware, the replay advances by $\Delta$ and drops intervening frames as a live camera would, so slower systems see sparser frames and pay latency inside the accuracy result.  Predictions are held between cycles and scored per frame.

\paragraph{Metrics.} We follow the baseline's suite~\cite{priorsystem2026}: frame accuracy and macro-F1 (the mean of per-state frame F1, $F_1=2\,\mathrm{IoU}/(1+\mathrm{IoU})$); per-state IoU; event precision/recall (an interval is correct if it overlaps one of the same state); entry offsets (Entry MAE over all states, \textsc{in} entry med.\ for \textsc{inside}); and false alarms per hour (predicted active intervals overlapping no annotated one), plus a lead metric (\textsc{inside} onsets preceded by $\geq$2\,s of active state).  The replay runs each video at its native frame rate.  Per-hour rates count frames at 30\,fps (validation time overstated by 3\%, for all systems alike).  Intervals are paired 95\% cluster-bootstrap intervals (10{,}000 replications) that resample drives.

\subsection{Operational cost}
\label{sec:cost}

At 480px, p50/p95 latency is 83/90\,ms (\textsc{gate}), 136/149 (\textsc{ego}), 155/169 (\textsc{sign}) and 468/508\,ms (\textsc{desc}); patrol and active cycles run at p95 259\,ms (3.9\,Hz) and 239\,ms (4.2\,Hz), the heaviest path at 768\,ms.  ``Real time'' means keeping pace with a live camera (259\,ms is 7.8\,m at 30\,m/s), not an alert deadline.  Above idle, the VLM draws 28.6\,W and 3--26\,J per cycle, the detector 22.4\,W and 0.5\,J at 24\,ms.

\section{Calibrating Every System}
\label{sec:worldmodel}

\Cref{sec:results} ran each VLM with thresholds fitted to the 2B and the detector pipeline with its published constants.  Here every system is recalibrated by the same replay procedure, and the ranking reverses (\cref{fig:ranking}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_timeline.pdf}
\caption{Ground truth and five systems on one validation video, chosen to illustrate the effect; thumbnails mark the middle of each annotated state.  With the 2B's constants, C3E never leaves \textsc{approaching}; recalibrated, it tracks the zone.  The hand-tuned detector pipeline enters \textsc{inside} 5\,s early and cuts \textsc{exiting} short; recalibrated, it follows all four states.}
\label{fig:timeline}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Temporal hyper-parameters do not transfer.}  With the 2B's constants, the zero-shot C3E almost never reaches \textsc{inside} (IoU 0.007, recall 3.8\%) and looks useless.  Recalibrating the same four integers and two switches lifts it to macro-F1 0.515 and \textsc{inside} IoU 0.574, with model, prompts and videos unchanged (\cref{fig:timeline}).  The search covers $N\in\{3,5,7,9\}$, $K_\ast\in 1..N$ and the \textsc{ego}/\textsc{sign} switches (4{,}896 configurations, later extended to $N\in\{1,2,4\}$ without changing the optimum), selected on calibration macro-F1 over the 100 videos with recorded VLM evidence.  The optimum ($N{=}3$, $K{=}3/2/1$) is shorter and less hysteretic and disables \textsc{ego} (\cref{sec:analysis}).  Because each channel is recorded once and the state machine replayed, a sweep costs CPU-seconds instead of GPU-hours.

The same search moves the fine-tuned 2B by only +0.006 (0.453$\to$0.459): it rescues a model whose constants were wrong rather than rewarding flexibility.

\paragraph{The baseline too.}  The detector pipeline deserves the same treatment.  We record every component on every cycle of all 520 videos (detector counts before and after CLIP verification, global CLIP and colour-context scores) and replay the complete pipeline; its hand-tuned configuration reproduces the live run (0.473 vs.\ 0.470).  Searching its thresholds, EMA rate and duration limits on the 312 calibration videos (three grids of 4{,}800 configurations) raises validation macro-F1 from 0.470 to 0.551 ($+0.081$, $[+0.043,+0.111]$), with 92.5\% \textsc{inside} precision at 20.2 false alarms per hour: the strongest system we measure.  On the detector score alone, without CLIP, the same search gives 0.435$\to$0.530.

The procedure also isolates what fine-tuning buys: recalibrated on its own evidence, the base checkpoint reaches 0.383 on the search videos against 0.496 for the fine-tuned model, not the collapse it shows under the fine-tuned model's thresholds.

All choices are made on calibration data, VLM thresholds on the 100 videos with recorded VLM evidence and detector thresholds and counted tables on all 312, so the budgets favour the detector.  Given the VLM's budget (one grid, the same 100 videos), the recalibrated pipeline still reaches 0.522, $+0.052$ over its hand-tuned version ($[+0.038,+0.070]$) and level with C3E.

\paragraph{No configuration dominates.}  Calibrated, the world model and the detector pipeline tie on macro-F1 ($-0.036$, $[-0.080,+0.005]$) and fail differently: C3E leads \textsc{inside} IoU and entry MAE, the detector pipeline precision and nuisance (20.2 vs.\ 64.6 false alarms/h).  \Cref{sec:joint} combines them.

\section{Combining the Detector and the World Model}
\label{sec:joint}

The joint estimator reaches 0.546 at 25.9 false alarms per hour: it beats the hand-tuned pipeline ($+0.076$, $[+0.038,+0.104]$) and the calibrated world model ($+0.031$, $[+0.006,+0.058]$), and ties the recalibrated pipeline ($-0.005$, $[-0.046,+0.033]$), trading precision for recall.

Its tables, counted on all 312 calibration videos (5-fold cross-validation 0.535, s.d.\ 0.019), are sparse but concentrated (20 of 108 cells carry 97\% of the observations); 3 or 10 detector buckets give 0.538 and 0.545, and $\tau$ trades accuracy for nuisance (0.538 at 44.7/h for $\tau{=}0.25$, 0.504 at 22.0/h for $\tau{=}1$).

\paragraph{Concurrent execution.}  The recorded runs behind every result had a 44\,ms detector cycle (p50; p95 59\,ms) and a world-model answer every 1.54\,s.  Running both models truly concurrently on the Orin, in wall-clock time from a decoded camera stream, on all 208 validation videos, raises the detector cycle from 24\,ms alone to 53\,ms (p95 58\,ms, 19\,Hz) with an answer every 1.44\,s.  Live, the joint estimator scores 0.548 against 0.546 in replay ($+0.002$, $[-0.011,+0.020]$; false alarms $+0.6$/h, $[-4.7,+5.6]$) and the detector-only filter 0.522 against 0.524: the replay is faithful under real GPU contention.  The Orin draws 39.0\,W median (46.9\,W peak) with both models, 33.5\,W with the detector alone and 11.1\,W idle.

\paragraph{Where the gain comes from.}  The detector carries the filter: without it macro-F1 drops by 0.086 and \textsc{exiting} IoU from 0.160 to 0.017, while removing the world model costs 0.022 and lowers alarms (25.9 to 22.5 per hour).  The detector score alone in the counted filter scores 0.524 at 22.5 false alarms per hour, tied with the recalibrated detector+CLIP pipeline on both ($-0.026$, $[-0.062,+0.009]$; $+2.2$/h, $[-8.1,+11.1]$) without CLIP or a threshold search, and 11.2 alarms per hour below the state machine recalibrated on the same score ($[-24.6,-2.1]$): the most efficient option here.  The world model pays where the detector is weak: on the 10 validation onsets preceded by a transcribed work-zone sign, the joint estimator and C3E warn $\geq$2\,s ahead on all ten, the recalibrated detector+CLIP pipeline on eight.

\paragraph{A learned alternative.}  A causal three-stage TCN~\cite{farha2019mstcn} on the same evidence ties the best macro-F1 (0.550) at 71.9 false alarms per hour.

\section{Do the Conclusions Hold?}
\label{sec:dd}

\paragraph{Unseen drives.}  Splitting the 25 drives into five folds of about 104 videos, we refit the detector state machines (full three-grid search) and the counted filters on four folds and replay them on the fifth, so no test video shares a drive with its fitting data (\cref{tab:dd}); the VLM cascades cannot be re-searched per fold, as per-channel evidence exists only for 100 calibration videos.  The conclusions hold: recalibration raises the detector+CLIP pipeline by 0.054 macro-F1 ($[+0.026,+0.078]$) and the detector-score state machine by 0.089 ($[+0.058,+0.114]$), and both counted filters tie the recalibrated pipeline ($-0.010$, $[-0.036,+0.017]$; $+0.002$).  On the validation videos, drive-disjoint fitting moves each refitted system's macro-F1 by at most 0.010 from \cref{tab:main}.

\paragraph{Matched nuisance.}  Systems operate at 18--65 false alarms per hour.  With one causal persistence filter applied to all (an active interval is shown only after lasting $d$ seconds) until false alarms fall to 20/h (last column of \cref{tab:main,tab:dd}), the calibrated systems lead: on unseen drives the recalibrated pipeline keeps 0.535, the joint estimator 0.523 and the counted detector filter 0.520, against 0.42 for the hand-tuned pipeline.  Matching nuisance also reorders anticipation (Lead@20 in \cref{tab:main}): the VLM systems need $d{\approx}3$\,s and the recalibrated pipeline 1.5\,s to reach 20/h, which delays their warnings, while the counted detector filter needs 0.25\,s and leads with 72\%.

\paragraph{Another platform.}  We reran four systems on a DRIVE AGX Thor with their Orin constants (published, calibrated or counted; \cref{tab:thor}).  Answers agree with the Orin's on 96--100\% of identical frames, but every stage is faster, so cycle-counted constants span less time and the loss tracks how much each cycle shrank: C3E's patrol cycle (\textsc{gate}+\textsc{sign}) fell from 718 to 240\,ms and it loses 0.042 macro-F1, the detector's from 24 to 16\,ms ($-0.031$), the 2B's only from 238 to 184\,ms ($-0.007$).  The joint estimator's loop shrank most, from 44 to 12\,ms, yet with per-second rates and $dt$-weighted evidence it keeps its macro-F1 (90\% interval $[-0.015,+0.024]$); its alarms rise by 37\% like the 2B's, against two thirds for the other two.  The detector-only filter was not run on the Thor.

\section{Failure Analysis: the Ego-Relevance Gap}
\label{sec:analysis}

Most residual false positives we inspected are correct descriptions of objects irrelevant to the vehicle's path (a parked work vehicle 60\,m away, sidewalk work behind barriers, detour signs for another street); the qualifier filter catches only what the model verbalizes, and binary or distance prompts do not recover the judgment.

\paragraph{Response bias.}  Recording the three checkpoints on the same videos separates how often a model says ``yes'' from how well it tracks the state (\cref{tab:bias}).  The base 2B rarely says yes outside a zone and separates \textsc{inside} from \textsc{outside} best (balanced accuracy 0.84); fine-tuning moves the gate to 42.4\% and 0.75; since ROADWork's \textsc{outside} includes visible but irrelevant work, such a ``yes'' is often correct perception without ego-relevance.  The fine-tuned model's advantage comes from its description, which corroborates on 10.7\% of \textsc{outside} seconds against 31.3\% for the base model.

C3E, never fine-tuned, transcribes signs correctly yet labels most verified \textsc{inside} frames ``off-road/sidewalk'' in ego-lane probes: perceiving objects and judging whether they claim the ego path are distinct capabilities, and no prompt we tried bridged them in either model.  The joint estimator reaches 0.160 \textsc{exiting} IoU only through the detector, whose score decays as objects recede (0.017 without it).  ROADWork's 129K path annotations~\cite{ghosh2025roadwork} could supply trajectory-grounded ego-relevance labels.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our claims are bounded in four ways.  \emph{(i) Data:} all footage is ROADWork daytime driving; drive-disjoint folds cover the detector state machines and counted filters but not the VLM cascades, and the 2B's absolute accuracy may benefit from drive overlap with its fine-tuning images (\cref{sec:protocol}).  \emph{(ii) Baseline:} our port scores 0.470 against a published 0.600, but our macro-F1 applied to the published per-state IoUs (0.56/0.11/0.47/0.09) gives 0.43, so the published figure differs in aggregation or split, and on our metric the port is at least on par with the original.  \emph{(iii) Search:} configurations are the best found on finite grids; with matched budgets the baseline still gains $+0.052$ and ties C3E.  \emph{(iv) Scope:} false alarms are measured mostly on videos containing work zones (13 of 208 are pure-negative), replay is open-loop, and we claim computational feasibility, not safety readiness.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study yields three insights for embedded perception.  First, temporal constants belong to a system, not to a task: inherited across models they move \textsc{inside} IoU by 0.567, and carried to faster hardware they cost up to 0.04 macro-F1 and add false alarms.  Second, the baseline must be calibrated like the proposed system: doing so lifts the detector pipeline from 0.470 to 0.551 and removes the lead the VLM-based systems held over its published version, and a counted filter on the detector score alone is statistically tied with it on unseen drives.  Third, on new hardware the loss of cycle-counted systems tracks how much their cycle shrinks, while the counted joint filter, whose parameters are per second, keeps its macro-F1 without recalibration although its loop shrinks most; its false alarms rise too.

The practical implication is direct.  Teams comparing an embedded VLM with a detector pipeline should recalibrate every system on the target hardware and compare at matched nuisance, and designers of temporal estimators should state their constants in seconds.  The VLMs' remaining gap is ego-relevance, which trajectory-grounded supervision may close; night and adverse-weather evaluation is the next step.  Upon acceptance we release the replay harness, prompts, metrics and baseline port.
